CHECKPOINT 11 — Odds ingestion and identity resolution
The math here is trivial. The hard part is that your odds and your match data use completely different names for the same people, and nothing will work until that is solved. I have looked at both schemas and the specifics are below — hand them over, because the agent cannot see your CSV files.

Checkpoint 10 approved. Now load the captured odds. The arithmetic here is easy; the identity matching is the real work, so treat it that way.

Create etl/odds/snapshots.py to load the nightly CSV archive in odds_snapshots/tennis_props_YYYY-MM.csv into the existing odds table. The CSV columns are: captured_at, fixture_id, game_id, league, tournament, start_date, status, home_player, away_player, sportsbook, market_id, market, player_id, selection, normalized_selection, selection_line, points, price, is_main, grouping_key, odds_id, odds_timestamp.

Be aware of three shape mismatches between the CSV and the odds table, and handle each explicitly. First, the CSV has one row per selection, so Over and Under are separate rows that must be pivoted into a single odds row with over_price and under_price. Match them on (fixture_id, sportsbook, market, player_id, points). Second, the CSV's points column is the line; map it to odds.line. Third, the CSV's odds_id is the odds provider's identifier and must not be written to odds.odds_id, which is a generated primary key — preserve the provider id in a new nullable provider_odds_id column added by a migration.

The identity problem. The CSV's player_id and fixture_id come from the odds provider. Your matches and players tables use Sackmann identifiers. They will never match, and the odds_match_player_fk foreign key will reject every row until you bridge them. Build an explicit resolution step that maps provider player names to your player_id values, reusing the provider_player_name / resolved_at pattern already established in migration 008 rather than inventing a second mechanism.

Unresolved names must be recorded, not dropped. Write them to a table or report with the provider name, the league, and how many rows were affected, so I can add mappings by hand. Print the top unresolved names by row count at the end of every run. A silent drop here means quietly missing odds forever, and I will not be able to tell the difference between "no line offered" and "we failed to match the name."
